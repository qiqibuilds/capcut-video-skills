# Shape choices

Offer these as a starting set. Show the sketches to the user when choosing a pattern. Scale, rotate, or mirror the selected shape to fit the footage while keeping its recognizable structure. The sketches guide the overall arrangement; their illustrated star counts do not determine the animated route. Choose exactly one connected star for each word, then draw one open line through those stars in reading order. Other stars remain unconnected decoration of the same size. Leave enough room beside each connected star for the full word.

## Big Dipper — recommended for the tutorial look

Seven stars in the familiar sketch: a bent three-star handle attached to a four-corner bowl. Use as many of those positions as the phrase needs for its connected word stars; leave the rest unconnected. Suggest the handle and bowl without tracing every edge or closing its rim.

```text
★──★╮
     ★──★────★
        │    │
        ★────★
```

## Orion

An hourglass outline with three close stars across the middle as the belt. The belt is the important visual cue; keep it visible even when only five words are used.

```text
★         ★
 ╲       ╱
  ★─★─★
 ╱       ╲
★         ★
```

## Northern Cross

Five main stars form a long stem and short crossbar. This works well with a five-word title.

```text
    ★
    │
★───★───★
    │
    ★
```

The Big Dipper is an asterism within Ursa Major; Orion and Cygnus/Northern Cross are recognizable sky patterns. See [NASA on asterisms](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/what-are-asterisms/), [NASA on Orion](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stories/quick-reads/discovering-the-universe-through-the-constellation-orion/), and [NASA on the Northern Cross](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/whats-up-august-2022/).
